# VENTURE CASE

**31 July 2026.** Written after the founder set the objective: solo-built, aggressively
pursuing accelerators and venture funding. This document does not relitigate the viability
review — it states what has to be true for that objective, where the current evidence
falls short, and what changes in the plan.

---

## 1. FIRST, A CORRECTION

`OPERATIONS-BRIEF.md` §4 argued for keeping the support burden small. That was about
headcount, not ambition, and it is worth restating in the opposite direction because it is
one of the strongest parts of this story:

**A product that absorbs its own support is the precondition for a solo-built company at
scale.** Revenue per employee is currently one of the metrics investors respond to most
strongly, and the AI-native founder running a real business without a team is a narrative
that raises money in 2026. The brief answering the question before a ticket is filed is
not a cost-saving measure — it is the demonstration that the product scales without
people. Instrument it: support contacts per hundred incidents handled, and watch it fall.

---

## 2. THE HONEST GAP

The viability review scored venture-scale potential **4/10** and concluded: $10M credible,
$100M conditional, $1B unsupported. That score measured **evidence, not ceiling.** Nothing
in the research says this cannot be big; what the research says is that the proof does not
exist yet. Three specific gaps, each of which is closeable:

| Gap | Why it holds the score down | What closes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurring behaviour is unproven** | Every verified purchase in this market is episodic rescue. The founder's own daily-change habit is n=1 | Cohort data: tasks per app per month, and quiet-month retention, from ten paying accounts |
| **The market is sized on today's apps** | Tens of thousands of production AI-built apps with real users [SI] — a niche, not a venture market | A defensible reframe (§3) plus evidence the pool is compounding |
| **BYO caps ARPU** | ~$99 flat, no expansion path, excludes owners without an AI subscription | Managed fuel, and the agency/multi-app tier where ACV is 5–10× |

The useful consequence: **the validation plan and the fundraise plan are the same plan.**
The 60-day gate in `BUILD-BRIEF.md` produces exactly the cohort data a seed investor asks
for. Nothing needs to be built twice.

## 3. THE STRONGEST TRUE VERSION OF THE MARKET STORY

The framing in the founder documents — monitoring and care for AI-built apps — describes a
niche and will be read as one. The larger framing is true, defensible, and better:

> **Software creation has become cheap; software operation has not.** Every year more
> applications exist that carry real users and revenue, and fewer of them have anyone
> qualified to run them. Selvedge is the operations layer for software whose owner cannot
> read it.

Why this holds up under questioning, with the evidence behind each leg:

- **Why now.** AI builders went from nothing to a combined $1.5B+ in annualised revenue in
  roughly two years (Lovable ~$500M, Replit ~$525M, Base44 ~$100M, Cursor ~$2B). None of
  them solved the after. The gap is created by their success and grows with it.
- **The gap is structural, not temporary.** Platforms bill consumption, so they profit
  when repair churns; they narrate their own work, so they cannot be trusted about their
  own failures. Those are incentive conflicts, not missing features — which is why Replit
  shipped monitoring in April 2026 and still cannot tell a customer they are overspending.
- **The precedent is real.** WordPress created a mature care-plan economy at $50–300/month
  per site that has lasted fifteen years. AI-built applications are more complex, more
  fragile, and more likely to carry money — and there are already more of them being
  created per week than WordPress sites at comparable maturity.
- **The substitute is a person.** Freelancers at $200/hour, agencies, fractional CTOs at
  $3–15K/month. That is the budget being displaced, and it is large and badly served.

**What not to claim.** Do not assert a TAM derived from "number of apps built with AI" —
most are abandoned within months, an investor who has done the work will know it, and
being caught inflating the top of the funnel costs more than a smaller honest number. The
credible construction is: production apps with real users, times a realistic capture rate,
times ARPU — and then the compounding argument for why that base grows faster than the
market's ability to staff it.

## 4. WHAT $10M / $100M / $1B ACTUALLY REQUIRES

| Target | Shape | What must be true |
|---|---|---|
| **$10M ARR** | ~10K accounts at ~$99, or a blend with agencies | Recurring behaviour proven; one repeatable acquisition channel. **Reachable on BYO alone** |
| **$100M ARR** | ~30K accounts at a $250 blended ARPU, or ~8K agency/team accounts at $1K+ | Managed fuel works economically; the agency segment converts; net revenue retention above 100% via app-count expansion |
| **$1B ARR** | The operations layer for small-business software generally, not only AI-built | The market reframe in §3 comes true, and Selvedge becomes the default answer to "who runs this?" — plus enterprise-lite and international |

The honest read: **$10M is a good business and is within reach of the current plan.
$100M requires the ARPU work — managed fuel and agencies — to start inside the first
year, not later.** That is the single biggest change the venture objective forces, and it
is a sequencing decision, not a product change: build BYO first because it wins the
refugees, but instrument and price for the expansion path from day one rather than
retrofitting it after the seed round.

---

## 5. METRICS TO INSTRUMENT FROM THE FIRST CUSTOMER

Investors ask for cohort data that cannot be reconstructed retroactively. Build the
instrumentation into Phase 1, not Phase 5.

**Growth and retention:** weekly new accounts; logo retention by cohort month; **net
revenue retention** (the expansion number — apps added per account over time is the
natural driver here); quiet-month cancellation rate specifically.

**Engagement — the thesis metric:** *tasks requested per app per month.* This is the number
that proves care is recurring rather than episodic. If it holds above 3 across a cohort,
the central objection to this company dissolves and that single chart carries the deck.

**Efficiency — the solo-founder story:** support contacts per hundred incidents;
**revenue per employee** (a number of one makes this extraordinary and it should be said
out loud); gross margin by fuel type.

**Product quality — the moat evidence:** repair convergence rate; **cost per successful
task by account tenure** — if that curve bends downward, the memory moat is real and
measured rather than asserted, which is the difference between a slide and an argument.

**Acquisition:** CAC by channel; payback period; rescue-to-subscription conversion.

## 6. ACCELERATORS SPECIFICALLY

What they weigh, and where this stands:

- **Founder who ships.** Three production codebases built solo, with a real product live
  in the App Store. This is the strongest card in the hand and it is evidence, not
  narrative. Lead with it.
- **Usage over vision.** Ten paying customers with the engagement chart beats any deck.
  The 60-day gate is the application.
- **The solo-founder question.** Accelerators prefer teams and will ask directly. The
  honest answer in 2026 is a strong one — an AI-native founder shipping at team velocity —
  but have a real answer about who joins and when, because "nobody, ever" reads as a
  ceiling rather than as efficiency.
- **Demo quality.** For a product whose thesis is that the interface makes or breaks it,
  the demo *is* the argument. The live loop — something breaks, it's explained in plain
  language, repaired, verified, recorded — is unusually demonstrable. Most infrastructure
  companies cannot show anything.
- **Timing.** Batch cycles are fixed and the competitive window is 6–15 months. Work
  backwards from the batch you are targeting; the gate data needs to exist before the
  application, not after.

---

## 7. WHAT CHANGES IN THE BUILD PLAN

Five adjustments, none of them large:

1. **Instrumentation moves to Phase 1.** The metrics in §5 cannot be backfilled.
2. **The agency tier moves from "market #2, later" to a deliberate pilot inside the first
   six months.** It is where ACV and net revenue retention live, and it is the only channel
   the platforms cannot absorb. One or two design partners, not a product line.
3. **Managed-fuel economics get tested early**, even if the launch is BYO. Whether it works
   at scale is the gating question for the $100M case, so learn it while it is cheap.
4. **Security and compliance move up one notch.** `OPERATIONS-BRIEF.md` §6 puts SOC 2 at
   year three; a funded company selling to teams and agencies meets that question sooner.
   Start the evidence trail — access logs, the append-only ledger, documented data handling
   — in Phase 1, when it is nearly free.
5. **The narrative gets built alongside the product.** Content about failed AI-built apps
   is both the primary acquisition channel and the fundraise story. Written once, used
   twice.

---

## 8. THE ONE THING THAT DECIDES IT

Everything above is preparation. The company becomes fundable at the moment a cohort of
real owners hands over the keys and keeps paying in months when nothing breaks —
demonstrating that this is an operations budget rather than an insurance impulse.

That is one chart: **tasks per app per month, and retention, over sixty days.** If it
holds, the venture case is no longer conditional and the score moves from 4 to something
defensible. If it does not, no amount of framing fixes it, and the honest outcome is a
profitable small company — which is a good result that should not be dressed as a
different one.

Build the slice. Get the chart.
