# Changelog

What changed, in the same words the commits use. An entry ships with every
deploy that changes behaviour; an empty month here is the thing this page
exists to prevent.

## 24 August 2026

- Sign in with GitHub or Google: the same two doors on the web and the phone,
  with email still there for everyone else.
- Arrive through GitHub and Selvedge greets you as your GitHub self: your
  repos, freshest first, and one step from each becoming a project.
- Import from Replit: the zip a Repl exports becomes a project around a
  private repo **you** own. Workspace junk is left behind by name, and if the
  files fail to land after the repo exists, the retry is offered and layers a
  commit rather than duplicating anything.
- Import from Cursor: `selvedge import cursor` reads the file Cursor actually
  keeps its chats in, on your machine, through the companion. What cannot be
  read is counted and listed — never silently dropped.
- Make the database yours: a database Selvedge provisioned can be claimed into
  your own Neon account from the project card. Connection strings survive the
  move, and Selvedge keeps nothing.
- The accounts behind a project are doors on its card: the Railway variables,
  the database console, the repo.
- Home is a question rather than a dashboard: ask at the top, jump back in
  underneath.
- A conversation names itself after the first thing you say in it, until you
  name it yourself.
- Search looks where you are before it looks everywhere, and says which it
  did: "found nothing in ringrunner, so searched everything" and "found this
  in ringrunner" are different answers.
- A project that cannot be previewed says what it is instead: an Xcode project
  gets a sentence, not a directory listing.
- 111 incidents that never happened are deleted, and a test now walks every
  event the tripwire counts as "users affected" through the real templates,
  so a contradiction can only be declared about something that contradicts.

## 23 August 2026

- Five more models answer to `@`: Gemini, Kimi, Grok, DeepSeek and Mistral.
  They arrived as five table rows rather than five integrations, because every
  provider but Anthropic speaks the same protocol at a different address.
- A consultation that names more models than it can ask now says who it left
  out. Naming six and hearing from three in silence was survivable when there
  were four agents in the room.
- The landing stops shipping the app it is describing: everything behind a
  session is split per route, so a first visitor no longer downloads a
  workbench they cannot open.
- Set, not just typed: real apostrophes, tabular figures, balanced headings,
  and a test that will still be switched on in a year.
- Panes hold their shape while they fill. Shaped skeletons, a 150ms floor so
  the shape never flashes, and an honest sentence past eight seconds instead of
  a shimmer that goes on forever.
- An empty screen is an invitation to act: every pane that can be empty now
  says what it is for and offers one thing to do.
- Nothing technical reaches a person. A network failure, a bodiless 502, and an
  HTML error page all become sentences, at the one seam they pass through.
- Every route says what it is, and a pasted link unfurls the product rather
  than a retired one.
- One product, one sentence: the documents and the landing page catch up with
  what the code has been for months.
- A place you are not working in can be put away. It leaves the rail and
  nothing else: still watched, still reachable by `#`, and the count of what
  is folded is always on screen.
- Codex builds on the key you connected, not the one in the environment.
- A long paste becomes an attachment rather than a wall of text, on both the
  web and the phone.

## 22 August 2026

- A build turn says why it failed, instead of leaving a thread reading
  "Working on it…" for forty-five minutes.
- Plain English reaches back: a question can name another conversation without
  knowing its exact title, and the database does the finding.
- `#references` — one conversation can read another, including a chat you had
  in ChatGPT last March.
- A turn can be stopped, and every answer says who gave it.
- Clone with the credential that can see the repo. Watching and building had
  drifted onto two different identities, which is why a project could be
  listed, described, and then fail at `git clone`.
- An old history belongs to the account, not to a project.
- $0.000 is not a price — a cost too small to write is now said in words.
- Take the wall down: any agent, any conversation, named in the sentence. The
  agent picker became the `@` menu, and the price of a handover is quoted
  before it is taken.
- A conversation can no longer spend past what you agreed to.
- The daily brief is retired; status moves in with the projects it is about.
- The rail becomes one list. A subject is a project without a repo, and you
  should not have to know which you are in before starting a conversation.
- Pressing + starts the conversation instead of asking a question first.

## 21 August 2026

- The Inbox and the Loop, phases 0–5.

  change — or a plain statement that it can't tell which.
- Consumer-history import: ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini exports become ordinary

  that is what they are.
- Delete the Work surface: every part of it had a better home.

## 20 August 2026

- Visible memory: a project's history, in one list, in plain sentences.
- The Inbox — a project has conversations, and you can change who is answering
  mid-task.

## 8 August 2026

- Sign-up, and the walkthrough that starts the moment it succeeds.
- First run: a welcome instead of a trap.
- Durable GitHub handshake, and honest retry accounting.

## 7 August 2026

  a different model than wrote the change reads the actual diff.
- `EVAL_MODEL` was setting the model that WROTE the code, not the one judging
  it.
- The health monitor was polling an empty table in production.
- Login with Railway: the module had no callers, so this is the other half.
